**Status:** Bounded discovery disposition

“Can” records an observed mechanism or workaround. “Should” is the durable design disposition after ownership, correctness, compatibility, diagnostics, hot-path cost, and lifecycle are considered.

## Decision summary

The selected PTX direction remains viable for correctness, static direct binding, coarse modules, and as the final device artifact boundary. Its durable form is not “hand-write PTX and accept every direct call,” and putting function bodies in one PTX input is not evidenced fusion. CUDA-MCGS should retain unbound disappearance, deterministic pre-ignition composition, and final-binary evidence as hard requirements, while separating semantic extension points from their realization: hot micro-policies are candidates for owned fusible lowering into the generated core; sufficiently coarse modules or stages are candidates for relocatable-PTX calls. CUDA-JS should add only the concrete consumer-neutral compile capability demonstrated missing here: relocatable device code. No additional CUDA-JS feature was justified by the follow-up. Linux remains an explicit two-owner sequence rather than a Windows workaround disguised as portability.

CUDA-MCGS follow-up is tracked in [UMCGS #30](https://github.com/iteathen/UMCGS/issues/30).
